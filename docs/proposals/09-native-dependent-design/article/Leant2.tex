\begin{abstract}
Leant2 should not be a transliteration of a Haskell inhabitation engine. It should be a native Lean elaboration-and-search system that constructs a program together with its behavioral evidence, retaining dependent types, universe levels, local instance identities, and metavariable dependencies throughout search. This article specifies such a system: a transactional native goal forest; bidirectional introductions, application-spine search, and dependent elimination; indexed component retrieval; fair portfolio scheduling; structural and well-founded recursive sketches; specification-directed branch synthesis; counterexample-guided finite backends; and kernel-checked publication under an explicit axiom and executability policy.

The central engineering separation is between semantic authority, search representation, and operational execution. Native expressions are authoritative, but open expressions alone are not portable search states. A proof-producing tactic may establish a type without supplying the desired behavior; a kernel-accepted recursor term may fail code generation; finite observational equality is not a safe permanent quotient; and exhaustion of a propositional or bounded synthesis fragment is not a proof that a Lean type is empty. Each boundary receives a concrete protocol and a precisely scoped guarantee.

Two included Lean-only prototypes were compiled remotely with Lean 4.34.0. One exercises dependent synthesis, shared-goal rollback, higher-rank callbacks, indexed elimination, and author-supplied recursive sketches. The other synthesizes an eight-node Boolean XOR expression using five counterexample-guided proposals and proves its universal Boolean specification by kernel reduction. These experiments validate narrow mechanisms, not the complete architecture or a broad coverage claim. The article supplies implementable algorithms, a module plan, benchmark gates, and a staged path to practical coverage without importing Haskell's type restrictions into the successor.
\end{abstract}
