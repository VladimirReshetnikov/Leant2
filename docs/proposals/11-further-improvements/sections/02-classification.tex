\section{Constraints and classification}\label{sec:classification}

\subsection{Constraints that stay}

The owner's requirements and the decisions of the unified proposal are not reopened here.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One adaptive engine, no settings.} Every improvement must decide for itself when it applies. A technique that needs the user to choose a mode is out of scope; a technique that needs a tuning constant must come with a way to set the constant from the query.
\item \textbf{The kernel is the only authority.} A candidate is published only after the exact program and the exact contract proof pass \code{addDeclCore} and the axiom audit. Heuristics, evaluators, learned models and external tools may propose and may prune; they never certify.
\item \textbf{Pruning must be sound with respect to the contract.} A branch may be abandoned only when no completion of the partial program can satisfy the contract, or when the loss is an accepted incompleteness of a bounded search (depth, splits). The first kind needs an argument; Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} returns to this.
\item \textbf{Preserve regression evidence.} The six historical harnesses of Table~\ref{tab:scores} retain their original revision and scope. An implementation change must also run the maintained acceptance suite at both 10 s and 5 s; an old score is not a current-source receipt.
\item \textbf{Core-only by default.} The toolchain is Lean~4.34.0 without Mathlib. Mathlib, Aesop, Plausible and similar packages may become optional lanes, never requirements.
\item \textbf{Replayable logical work.} A fixed query, environment, grammar, policy and proposal transcript should reproduce a recorded logical search prefix and its candidates. Equal wall-clock budgets need not complete the same prefix. Parallel result sorting alone cannot remove differences in discovery.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Three classes of work}

\begin{description}
\item[\cls{E} Engineering.] The algorithm is known and has been used in a comparable setting. The risk is effort and integration.
\item[\cls{A} Adaptation.] The algorithm is known for simply typed or ML-like languages, or inside Lean for proofs rather than programs. It must be re-derived for dependent types, universe polymorphism, type classes, and a search whose state is a metavariable context. The risk is that the adaptation loses the property that made the original work.
\item[\cls{R} Research.] We do not know of an algorithm that is optimal, or even clearly adequate, for the problem as it arises here. These are the places where time spent reading, experimenting and talking to specialists is likely to pay more than time spent coding.
\end{description}

The research items share machinery without having identical obligations.
R2 and R5 both invent state or helper interfaces absent from the specification;
recursive realization also needs termination, scope and interpreter-agreement
arguments. R3 can make both searches more affordable through conservative
observations. R1 allocates their work within the interactive budget.
Useful bounded carriers, native induction and sketches need not wait for an
unrestricted carrier-minimization algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:roadmap} gives
the revised dependency order.
