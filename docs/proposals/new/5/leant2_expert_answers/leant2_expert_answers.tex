\begin{abstract}
The further-improvements proposal for Leant2 poses 31 expert questions in nine areas: search control, carrier invention, evaluation with holes, dependent elimination, general recursion, contracts, retrieval, ranking, and learned guidance. This article gives a separately identifiable answer to every question, grounded in a pinned repository snapshot, primary literature, and explicit mathematical arguments. Several apparent research barriers have useful bounded solutions: finite-state carrier inference becomes a finite constraint problem; type classes already have a dictionary-passing treatment in Hoogle+; and Lean exposes a concrete motive-construction API. Other ambitions need sharper contracts: finite observations do not determine a unique semantic carrier, open-goal counts are not generally admissible search bounds, and final kernel checking cannot repair an unsound pruning rule.

The proposed architecture keeps Lean's native expressions and transactional state, adds dependency-indexed constraints and replayable proposal recipes, and separates fixed certification objectives from adaptive search priorities. It gives substitution-stable residual checking, capability-based recursion with a simulation obligation, local inductive contracts, and a retrieval abstraction that preserves dependencies conservatively. The recommendations include amendments to the acceptance boundary and result algebra, a staged implementation plan, and reproducible finite-model checks. No new Leant2 performance measurements or compiled Lean implementation are claimed.
\end{abstract}
