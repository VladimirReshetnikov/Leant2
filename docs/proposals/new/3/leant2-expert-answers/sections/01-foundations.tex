\subsection{Five guarantees, not one}

Let a frozen query consist of an environment $E$, a target $T$, a contract $C:T\to\mathrm{Prop}$, and an allowed-axiom policy $A$. Let $G_B$ be the explicitly bounded candidate grammar. The following distinctions should appear in both implementation documentation and user-facing receipts.

\textbf{Acceptance correctness.} An accepted result includes closed expressions $p:T$ and $\pi:C(p)$, checked in $E$, with their transitive axiom dependencies permitted by $A$. This is a statement about a particular program and proposition. It says neither that the proposition fully captures the user's intent nor that a separately compiled implementation has been proved equivalent.

\textbf{Pruning correctness.} Deleting a search state must not delete a completion satisfying the contract. If $s$ represents a partial program and $\Comp(s)$ its possible completions, a sound refutation establishes
\[
  \forall p\in\Comp(s),\quad \neg C(p).
\]
A final kernel check cannot repair a valid solution that was discarded earlier. Unsound pruning may preserve correctness of every accepted answer while destroying completeness.

\textbf{Bounded completeness.} Every satisfying member of $G_B$ is eventually considered, or at least one is found whenever one exists, depending on the advertised service. This needs a finite, fully specified grammar, fair exploration, and correct rejection decisions. An unresolved type-checking or proof-search timeout is not evidence that an alternative is impossible.

\textbf{Ranking optimality.} The returned candidate minimizes a stated, fixed objective over a stated candidate set. A heuristic search priority, a term-size ranking, and a display preference are not automatically the same objective. An anytime answer need not be optimal even when every term in it is certified.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Proof replay, search replay, and reproduction of a model's inference are three distinct tasks. Rechecking the same closed proof can be reproducible even when another run discovers a different proof. Identical search events require a fixed environment, policy, proposal stream, resource accounting, and tie-breaking discipline.

The repository already embodies much of the acceptance discipline: \code{gate} instantiates terms, rejects unresolved holes and escaped locals, performs synchronous kernel checking, and audits axiom dependencies \cite{gate}. These checks are a good foundation. The remaining guarantees require additional invariants outside the gate.

\subsection{A source-level integration observation}

The present gate accepts an optional pair consisting of a proof and its claimed type. It does not itself receive the original contract. In the inspected engine, the caller correctly constructs that type as $C(p)$ from the synthesized subtype value. Thus the observation is \emph{not} a demonstrated soundness failure in the current pipeline. It is an interface-hardening opportunity: a future public gate should receive the frozen query and derive the expected program type and proof type itself. Receipts should preserve the original target and contract, rather than only a caller-supplied proof expression \cite{gate,engine,coretypes}.

Similarly, \code{Accepted} currently records the program, its type, universes, proof, axioms, and classical flag, but not the original contract. The implemented \code{Outcome} has no explicit ``unrefuted but uncertified'' constructor. The proposal's intended richer outcome algebra should therefore be implemented, not assumed already present \cite{coretypes}.

\subsection{Corrections that change the design}

The most consequential corrections are summarized here; the detailed arguments appear in the answers.

\midrule
\endhead
A* lower bounds & Counting open goals can overestimate remaining cost when exact moves cost zero or one assignment closes several goals. Use a proved relaxation, not the count alone.\\
Carrier expressibility & Reverse is a right fold into \code{List A}; a continuation carrier improves the operational form. Failure at a small search depth is not semantic impossibility.\\
Prior auxiliary-state work & AutoLifter directly synthesizes auxiliary information and combinators. The unsolved part is adapting such ideas to sparse contracts and Lean-dependent carriers, not the existence of auxiliary-state synthesis as a research topic.\\
Live evaluation & Smyth's implementation supports higher-order examples and removes trace completeness. Dependent typed closures remain a separate adaptation problem.\\
Indexed proof search & Canonical already demonstrates indexed recursion and a generalized-induction example. It is not evidence for an unrestricted complete motive search, but it is a concrete precedent.\\
Type classes & Hoogle+ includes dictionary translation. Lean's dependent instance constraints extend that problem; they do not introduce it from scratch.\\
Transport example & For Lean's natural addition, $n+0$ reduces to $n$. Use $0+n$ versus $n$ to illustrate a genuinely propositional index equality in the usual open-variable setting.\\
Deterministic results & Sorting after parallel search removes arrival-order presentation differences, not differences in which candidates were discovered.\\
