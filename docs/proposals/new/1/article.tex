\begin{abstract}
This article answers all 31 questions in the nine research sections of
\emph{Leant2: Design for Further Improvements}. It examines the proposal and
selected implementation modules at repository commit
\texttt{784664ff34e8}, checks relevant primary literature, and turns the
questions into explicit algorithms, correctness obligations, and experiments.
Several questions already have stronger published answers than the proposal
acknowledges: Smyth supports higher-order examples and incomplete recursive
examples; Hoogle+ handles type classes; auxiliary-state synthesis has concrete
precedents in AutoLifter, Para, and auxiliary-function synthesis; and Lean
exposes reusable motive-construction operations. Other questions need their
premises tightened. In particular, summed goal-count heuristics are not
necessarily admissible, finite observations do not identify a minimal carrier
type, proof irrelevance does not license arbitrary cast erasure, and a hard
wall-clock cutoff cannot generally preserve identical candidate sets across
machines. The proposed architecture separates certified acceptance,
conservative pruning, and heuristic guidance. It combines dependency-aware
persistent search, typed residual observations, bounded lifting templates,
proof-carrying recursion, and replayable decision traces. Mathematical
arguments and a small executable finite-model suite support the central
claims; no new Leant2 performance measurements or Lean-checked implementation
are claimed.
\end{abstract}
